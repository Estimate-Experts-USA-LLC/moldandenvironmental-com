Mold and mildew: common questions

The basics, without the sales pitch.

Mold and mildew, briefly

Both are fungi. In everyday use, mildew describes flat growth on a surface — the grey or white film on bathroom grout, window frames or a damp wardrobe — which usually wipes off and comes back if the conditions do not change.

Mold is used more broadly, including growth that penetrates the material it is on. That is the difference that matters practically: something growing into drywall or timber is not a cleaning job in the way a surface film is.

Neither is a precise scientific category, so do not put much weight on which word someone uses. Whether the material is affected through its thickness matters more than the label.

What we will not tell you

We will not tell you a building is "safe" or "mold free". Mold spores are present in ordinary indoor and outdoor air everywhere; a building with none is not a thing that exists, and a document claiming otherwise would be describing something no method can measure.

We will not interpret your symptoms. That is a doctor's job.

We will not give a firm remediation price over the phone without seeing the property. Anyone who does is guessing, and the number will change once someone opens the wall.

Health, honestly

What we can and cannot tell you

This page is general information about buildings, not medical advice. If you are worried about how a damp building may be affecting your health, speak to a doctor — they can assess a person, and we cannot.

Common questions

Questions we are asked

What is mold testing?

Taking air or surface samples and having an independent laboratory identify what is present and in what concentration. Air samples are compared against an outdoor control taken the same day, because without that comparison an indoor number means very little.

What is a mold inspection?

A visual and moisture-based examination of the property: where water is getting in, which materials are affected, and how far it goes. Sampling may or may not form part of it.

Do I need testing, or just an inspection?

If growth is visible and the moisture source is obvious, sampling often adds nothing. It earns its place when the extent is unclear, when a cavity cannot be opened, or when a written record is needed.

Is mildew on bathroom grout something to worry about?

It is usually a ventilation and humidity problem rather than a building-damage one. Improve extraction and it generally stops recurring.

How long does remediation take?

It depends on the area and the materials, and drying is often the longest part. We give an expected timescale before starting and tell you if it changes.

Do you work with insurance?

We can document what we found and what we did, and you may give that to your insurer. We do not advise on, negotiate or adjust claims — that is a matter for you and your insurer or a licensed public adjuster.

What areas do you cover?

Fort Pierce, Port St. Lucie, Vero Beach, West Palm Beach, Fort Lauderdale and the surrounding Florida areas.
