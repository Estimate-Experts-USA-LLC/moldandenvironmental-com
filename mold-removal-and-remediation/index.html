Before you book removal

When remediation is the right step

Removal is warranted when material is affected and cannot simply be cleaned — and when the moisture that caused it can be corrected. If either is not true, we will say so.

Affected material

Drywall, insulation, carpet, padding or timber that has taken on moisture and growth, where cleaning the surface will not resolve it.

A defined scope

Ideally a written protocol from an independent assessor, so the work has an agreed boundary and a way to be checked afterwards.

A fixable water source

A leak, a failed seal, condensation or drainage that can be corrected. Removal without correcting the water is temporary.

The process

How a remediation runs

Every project differs, but most follow this order. We will tell you before we start which steps apply to yours and roughly how long each takes.

01

Containment

The affected area is sealed off before anything is disturbed, so what is released stays in the room being worked on.

02

Air control

Negative air machines with filtration draw air out of the contained area rather than pushing it into the rest of the property.

03

Material removal

Affected material is cut out, bagged inside the containment and disposed of, so it is not carried back through the building.

04

Cleaning & drying

HEPA vacuuming and wiping of the exposed structure and salvageable contents, then drying the area before anything is closed back up.

HVAC

Where the system served the affected area, ducts and air handlers are cleaned — otherwise the system can move what you have just removed back through the property.

Contents

Hard surfaces and many contents can be cleaned. Porous items that absorbed moisture and growth often cannot, and we will tell you which is which rather than clean something that should be replaced.

The honest part

Removal does not fix a leak

Remediation deals with the material. It does not repair the roof, the plumbing or the drainage that let water in. If the water source is still there, growth can return — and no remediation company can promise otherwise.

We look for that source as part of the work and tell you plainly what needs correcting, including when it needs a different trade. You are better served knowing that up front than discovering it in six months.

How we can be engaged

Two routes, and we only take one per project

You already have a protocol

If an independent assessor has written a scope, we carry out the remediation to it under licence MRSR3880. Our project manager reviews the protocol with you and schedules the work.

You need the assessment first

We assess under licence MRSA3298 and give you a written protocol to take to a remediation contractor. On that project the removal is carried out by someone else.

Why not both

The company deciding how much work is needed should not be the company paid to do it. Florida licenses the two activities separately for that reason. We hold both licences, and we take one role per project.

Afterwards

Post-remediation verification

When our removal work is finished, verification is carried out by a third-party assessor — not by us. That is the point of it: the check is only meaningful if the people who did the work are not the people marking it.

If an area does not pass, we go back and redo it. Verification tests the areas that were worked on against the agreed scope; it is not proof that a building contains no mold anywhere, and nobody can produce that.

Common questions

Remediation questions

How long does remediation take?

It depends on the size of the affected area and the materials involved. A single bathroom wall is a different job from a whole floor after a flood. We give you an expected timescale before starting and tell you if it changes.

Can we stay in the property?

Often yes, because containment limits the work to one area. We will tell you before starting which rooms are unavailable and for how long, and we will say if we think you should be out for part of it.

Will the mold come back?

It can, if the moisture source is not corrected. That is the honest answer. Removal deals with the material; the leak or condensation problem has to be fixed separately or the conditions that caused it still exist.

Do you guarantee the mold is completely gone?

No, and we would be cautious of anyone who does. What we can do is remove the affected material to an agreed scope, clean and dry the area, document the work, and have it independently verified.

Do you do the testing afterwards?

Not on a project we remediated. Post-remediation verification is carried out by an independent assessor, which is what makes it worth having.
